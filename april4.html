A routine that is critical to my life is going to the gym. I’ve never been particularly athletic or good at any sports. Despite being the 2nd tallest girl in middle school I didn’t even make the junior basketball team. Besides for going on walks with my dog at home, exercise was never a core routine in my day. However, when I came to college I was met with a challenge I could never have anticipated.

I’ve always had too many hobbies to count. Between playing multiple instruments, participating in orchestra, and doing art on the side, having a creative outlet was never an issue for me. Whenever I would get overwhelmed with schoolwork I could always take comfort in the sketchbooks I had tucked away in my drawer or lose myself for an hour or two playing guitar. In college, these outlets were no longer available to me. As a Communication Design major, all I do everyday is art. While sometimes comforting, my main form of therapy has become one of my main stressors as deadlines, critiques, and assignments weigh on my shoulders. As for music, the first club I joined in college was an a cappella group. It has been an amazing source of joy in my life and I love the community I’ve built from it but simultaneously it takes up 7 hours of my week and when I get back to my dorm after a long day of class and then practice, I want nothing more than to fall into bed and sleep. I quickly realized after first semester that I needed a new outlet to release energy and forget about work for a few hours a day, and I turned to the gym.

By no means am I the most knowledgeable about working out. Half the time I barely do the exercise right and the other half of the time there is a 40 person line for the machine I want to use. But going to the gym allows me to turn off my brain for even 30 minutes and blast my music as loud as I possibly can. My brain is occupied by counting reps or singing along silently to the song in my earbuds so I can barely even form a thought. And even if I’m having a bad day or I’m not being productive, I can say at the end of the day that it wasn’t a total waste of the day because at least I worked out. When I first started working out consistently, I had to drag myself out of bed and every day it was a struggle. Now, it’s not even a second thought that it is a critical and essential part of my daily routine.
