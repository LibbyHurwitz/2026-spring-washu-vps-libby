For me, the effortful process of bracelet making has always been one of my favorite past-times. I started making friendship bracelets in sleep away camp when I was 11 years old and initially it was just a casual hobby to do with my friends or to trade before we left. During Covid I found an old box of string in my closet and decided to try making them again to pass the time while on endless zooms. For me, making a bracelet is a practice in intentionality. Each individual knot is essentially identical but every minute detail from the order of strings to the direction of the knot has a profound impact on the final product. I tend to work off of patterns when I make bracelets and for the majority of each project I don’t zoom out to see the guide image until the end. It’s a slow and deliberate process of knotting and organizing and detangling that allows me to direct all focus and creative energy. Now that I am a design major there is a huge focus on the end product that I create and the final critique and whether or not it can be added to my portfolio and less on the actual process of crating and creating. Bracelet making for me is a reminder of the value of the process and that sometimes it’s okay to just value the act of creating and not ensuring that everything is perfect.
